# EX-12: a supported opportunity, not yet an accumulated step

One online-born, pre-establishment B→C candidate passed the registered dependency
gates in both its birth-time ecology and standardized worlds. This is **limited
support**, not the registered replicated success: three independent discovery
seeds were required, and only one qualified. That candidate also left **zero
offspring in the original history**. We have evidence for a context-specific
selective opportunity, not a retained causal chain or open-ended evolution.

## What was done

The EX-11 ecology, resource barrier, online composition operator, mutation rates,
population size and 3,000-tick horizon were unchanged. EX-12 used 40 new discovery
worlds: ten seeds each for full evolution, B-only, no-substrate and atomic-only.
Candidate selection changed to the first clean B-lineage descendant acquiring
high uptake C, irrespective of establishment or subsequent reproductive success.
The protocol and evidence gates were recorded before fresh seeds were run.

For every selected candidate, we tested A, B, C-only and BC in two distinct
contexts: ten paired continuations from its serialized birth-time snapshot, and
ten separate standardized worlds with ancestral competitors. All these assays
disabled further mutation. The actual pre-B parent program supplied A/C-only.
Each context required B>A, BC>B and positive interaction in at least8/10 futures
and on average, with C-only<=A in at least8/10 and on average. No extinct lineage
or tie was dropped. These are registered screening gates, not calibrated p-values.

The complete record contains 40 discovery worlds, 320 factorial worlds, 80
factorial restorations, four original-history C reversions, four discovery
restorations and four untouched-snapshot continuation checks. Every selected
candidate was tested; none was replaced after failure.

## The supported case

In discovery seed `6840321394656415753` (offset+9), B was born at tick955 and its
clean descendant acquired C at tick1000. B was **45 ticks old**, and its clean
lineage occupied **32/256 slots (12.5%)** immediately before C's birth. The
online-generated enzyme was `add_sat(a,mask_xor(a,b))`, the same capability known
from EX-11, with a new evolutionary origin rather than a seeded genotype.

| Registered contrast | Historical opportunity | Functional dependency |
|---|---:|---:|
| B better than A | 9/10 | 9/10 |
| BC better than B | 10/10 | 10/10 |
| C-only no better than A | 10/10 | 10/10 |
| Positive interaction | 10/10 | 10/10 |

All required mean signs also passed. The mean C-with-B effect was +14,984.2
focal-lineage births in historical continuations and +11,320 in standardized
worlds. Without B, the mean C effect was -0.3 and -6.2, respectively. These are
200-tick descendant-lineage totals, not direct offspring or per-tick fitness.
The designed resource economics strongly favour high uptake after decoding;
this is not a newly discovered biological principle.

## Why the original-history result matters

The passing candidate was overwritten by another birth during the same birth
phase, before it could reproduce. Suppressing its C mutation made no difference
to its descendant count (0 versus0) or the original world's end-of-tick history
hash. A separate post-run audit of the saved pending-parent list and original
counter RNG identifies replacement child3852, parent3838, at slot119.

The historical-opportunity assay keeps the actual birth-time population, nutrient
stocks, location and newborn energy, but samples fresh future randomness and
disables mutation. It therefore establishes a **conditional reproductive
opportunity from that state**. It does not retroactively make the original child
successful, prove an advantage in the exact realized future, or establish
historical necessity of B.

This is the distinction the instrument now makes visible: a genotype can offer
a robust opportunity in controlled futures and still disappear immediately in
the history that actually occurred.

## The other contexts were different

Four first-clean candidates were selected; three preceded operational
establishment. The other pre-establishment candidates arose at clean-B fractions
of63.3% and79.3%, demonstrating why "before the50-tick rule" must not be treated
as synonymous with "rare." They failed the historical gate: B>A in4/10 and6/10,
and BC>B in7/10 for both. The already-established candidate also failed the
historical B>A gate (4/10), despite BC>B in9/10.

All four passed the standardized gate, but **they share the same genotypes and
the same standardized assay seeds**. These are40 distinct genotype/seed assays
executed four times, not160 independent functional tests or four different
innovations. Their historical snapshots are distinct and their effects are
reported separately. We do not pool the two contexts.

Two candidates did leave large descendant lineages in their original worlds,
and their C-only reversions eliminated those particular descendants. Neither
passed both assay contexts. The case passing both contexts was not one of the
historically successful cases. Choosing a later surviving mutation instead would
violate the registered first-candidate rule.

## Discovery was separated from retention

B appeared in5/10 full worlds and established and persisted in4/5 of those.
B-only had the same5/10 discovery and4/5 conditional retention. With no substrate,
B appeared in5/10 and established in0/5. Atomic-only acquired no capability.
These are small-sample, paired-arm observations, not precise probabilities or
proof that innovation supply is the sole bottleneck.

## What the causal ledger can claim

For the offset+9 edge, functional dependency and historical opportunity meet
their registered gates. Historical necessity of B and environmental mediation
remain **untested**. One conditional edge is supported; no two-edge chain has
been tested, and no accumulated retained depth is established by this result.
All four selected first-clean candidates were assayed, but coverage of every
potential causal relationship in these histories is unknown.

EX-12 closes without changing its gates. The next design decision is whether to
prioritize independent replication of nascent opportunities or the retention and
transmission of supported opportunities. A future retention-focused protocol
must select candidates prospectively and report attrition; it cannot rescue this
matrix by searching its later births for successful replacements. Physics,
ecological inheritance and mutation-policy evolution were not introduced.
