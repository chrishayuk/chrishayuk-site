# Cinematic laboratory notebook: references

The lab notebook describes the activity; exhibition describes its presentation.
Keep the squared-paper record, but let photographic plates, contact sheets and
occasional changes of scale give selected studies a stronger visual rhythm.

- **Magnum contact sheets** — https://store.magnumphotos.com/collections/contact-sheets
  and the Ransom Center's exhibited Bendiksen sheet:
  https://sites.utexas.edu/ransomcentermagazine/2013/10/29/in-the-galleries-jonas-bendiksens-contact-sheets/
  Working sequences and selected frames. Proposal: one inspectable frame per
  recorded test world, paired with its counterpart, keeping all twelve visible.
- **Gerhard Richter, Atlas** —
  https://www.gerhard-richter.com/en/exhibitions/gerhard-richter-atlas-19641995-721/museum-space-13197
  A collection of photographs, sketches and clippings becomes an exhibition.
  Proposal: mount actual records, source fragments and diagrams as a composed plate.
- **La Galerie Dior / Base Design** — https://www.galeriedior.com/about
  and https://www.basedesign.com/projects/dior
  Sketches, garments and archives receive individual settings. Proposal: vary
  scale, light and the amount of space around a notebook's key object.
- **IGNANT, Spaces Between** —
  https://www.ignant.com/2018/10/18/ignant-studios-first-print-magazine-spaces-between/
  A print reference for photographic storytelling. Proposal: photographed paper,
  captured screens and details of actual working objects introduce selected notes.
- **Hearst Exhibit 2026 / OSMOS** — https://www.osmos.co/project/hearst-exhibit-2026
  The designers describe a scroll-led digital gallery for covers and photographs.
  Proposal: give a chosen plate its own visual interval before the evidence spread.
- **V&A, Codex Forster III** —
  https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/explore-leonardo-da-vincis-notebooks-codex-forster-iii
  The working-surface reference: drawing, technical notes and varied subjects
  coexist on the page. This anchors the notebook rather than its cinematic pacing.

First proposed study: the repair notebook. Use an actual recorded artefact as the
opening object, then the four-number setup, twelve paired worlds, the 0/12 versus
7/12 comparison, and a closing reflection with methods and sources available.
Those counts compare arms, not a before/after improvement. A photographed object
or illustrative scene must identify what it is; it cannot stand in for a captured
experimental event. No new photography or scene sequence has been implemented.
